# Questions to Ask Them: [FULL NAME], [COMPANY], [ROUND LABEL]

*Your questions are the part of the round you fully control, and the part most candidates waste. This file is organised by what a question does for you, not by subject matter, with categories in recommended lead order.*

---

## Budget note, read this first

*In a thirty minute round you get roughly five minutes at the end, sometimes less. That is two questions, three if the answers run short. Do not plan to ask all of these. Pick two or three, mark them, and keep the rest as backup for when yours gets answered mid conversation.*

- Minutes I actually expect: [NUMBER]
- My first choice: [QUESTION]
- My second choice: [QUESTION]
- My backup if one gets answered early: [QUESTION]

---

## 1. Their load and priorities

*Lead here. It is the least self serving thing you can ask, it makes them describe their real week, and the answer usually contains the actual job. It also signals you are thinking about relieving pressure rather than being onboarded.*

- "[QUESTION ABOUT WHAT IS CURRENTLY EATING THEIR TIME]"
  *Signals: you think in terms of their workload, not your career path.*
- "[QUESTION ABOUT WHAT THEY WOULD HAND OFF FIRST TO A NEW HIRE]"
  *Signals: you are already scoping the first ninety days without being asked.*
- "[QUESTION ABOUT WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS HIRE FEEL OBVIOUSLY WORTH IT AT SIX MONTHS]"
  *Signals: you are willing to be measured.*

---

## 2. Roadmap and direction

*Second. They have described the present, so ask about the direction. Anchor these to something specific from your research so it does not read as a generic "what is the roadmap."*

- "[QUESTION TYING A RECENT PUBLIC MOVE TO WHAT COMES NEXT]"
  *Signals: you did real research and can connect a public fact to a product consequence.*
- "[QUESTION ABOUT WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT ON THE ROADMAP]"
  *Signals: you understand that strategy is what you say no to.*
- "[QUESTION ABOUT WHICH BET THEY ARE LEAST SURE OF]"
  *Signals: you can hold ambiguity without needing certainty.*

---

## 3. Their home turf

*Third, and only if you have the substance to hold the follow up. Mirror back the thing this person spent years getting good at. Done well it is the most memorable question in the round. Done badly it exposes that you skimmed a talk abstract. If your dossier says "searched, not found," skip this category.*

- "[QUESTION ABOUT A SPECIFIC TECHNICAL OR OPERATIONAL PROBLEM THEY HAVE PUBLICLY WRESTLED WITH]"
  *Signals: you engaged with their actual work, not their title.*
- "[QUESTION ABOUT WHAT THEY BELIEVE NOW THAT THEY DID NOT BELIEVE FIVE YEARS AGO IN THEIR DOMAIN]"
  *Signals: you treat them as a practitioner with a point of view.*

*Worked example (fictional): Priya Raghunathan, a staff engineer at Northwind Payments, gave a talk about cutting settlement reconciliation from overnight batch to near real time. A weak question is "tell me about your architecture." A strong one is "in the reconciliation move, was the hard part the throughput or the exception handling when a batch and a stream disagreed?" The second only works if you can respond intelligently to either answer.*

---

## 4. Team shape and ways of working

*Fourth. Useful but self oriented, so it goes after the questions that show you are thinking about them. Ask about mechanics you would have to live inside.*

- "[QUESTION ABOUT HOW A DECISION ACTUALLY GETS MADE WHEN TWO TEAMS DISAGREE]"
  *Signals: you know org friction is the real work.*
- "[QUESTION ABOUT HOW THE TEAM IS SPLIT AND WHO OWNS WHAT]"
  *Signals: you are mapping the terrain, not asking for reassurance.*
- "[QUESTION ABOUT WHAT A GOOD WEEK LOOKS LIKE FOR THIS ROLE]"
  *Signals: you want to know the job, not the perks.*

---

## 5. A warm personal closer

*Last, and short. One human question that ends the round on a real exchange rather than a logistics note. Keep it genuine and easy to answer. Do not ask anything that requires them to be vulnerable with a stranger.*

- "[QUESTION ABOUT WHAT MADE THEM STAY, OR WHAT SURPRISED THEM WHEN THEY JOINED]"
  *Signals: you are treating this as a conversation between two people.*
- "[QUESTION ABOUT WHAT THEY ARE MOST LOOKING FORWARD TO SHIPPING]"
  *Signals: warmth, and it usually gets an unguarded answer.*

---

## What makes a bad question

*Check every question against this list. If it trips one, cut it or rewrite it.*

- **Answered on the careers page.** If ten seconds of searching would have answered it, it shows you did not spend the ten seconds.
- **Secretly about you.** "What is the promotion path" is a fine question for the recruiter and a poor one for a hiring manager in a first round.
- **A statement wearing a question mark.** If the question exists so you can deliver an opinion, they will hear the opinion.
- **Unanswerable at their level.** Do not ask an individual contributor about the company's three year strategy or a recruiter about system architecture.
- **A trap.** Questions that make them defend a weakness put them on their heels and cost you the warmth you just built.
- **Already answered in the round.** Asking something they covered twenty minutes ago says you were not listening. This is why you carry backups.

## The version to have open during the call

*Copy the block below into a note you can see during the call. Short, no italics, no explanations, nothing to read past. This is the only version you look at live.*

```
ASK (pick 2, maybe 3):

1. [FIRST CHOICE QUESTION, ONE LINE]
2. [SECOND CHOICE QUESTION, ONE LINE]
3. [THIRD CHOICE QUESTION, ONE LINE]

BACKUPS (if one gets answered):
- [BACKUP QUESTION]
- [BACKUP QUESTION]

CLOSER (if time):
- [WARM PERSONAL QUESTION]

Note during their answers:
- how they describe the team
- the hardest problem they name
- any word they repeat
```
