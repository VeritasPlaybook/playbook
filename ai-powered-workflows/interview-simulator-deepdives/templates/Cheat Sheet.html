Open and Scripts

The first ninety seconds and the handful of lines you must not improvise.

Who I am, in twenty five seconds

Say this out loud, word for word [YOUR TWENTY FIVE SECOND OPENER HERE. Example shape: I am a product manager (PM) who has spent the last [N] years on [DOMAIN]. Most recently I [ONE CONCRETE THING YOU BUILT]. The reason I am here is [ONE SENTENCE, SPECIFIC TO THIS COMPANY].]
Stop Do not keep going into your full history. Say the opener, then stop and let them steer.

Why [COMPANY]

Say this out loud, word for word [YOUR WHY THEM LINE HERE. One specific thing about their product or their problem, not their slogan. Example, fictional: Northwind Payments is doing the unglamorous version of the work, moving reconciliation off overnight batch, and that is the part I actually like.]
Honest limit line [IF THEY PUSH ON SOMETHING YOU HAVE NOT DONE: I have not run [THING] at your scale. What I have done is [NEAREST REAL THING], and here is what transferred.]

Why I am looking

Say this out loud, word for word [YOUR ONE SENTENCE. Factual, no bitterness, no long explanation. Then stop.]
Stop One sentence only. The longer this answer runs, the worse it sounds. Silence after it is fine.

Close of the round

Say this out loud, word for word [YOUR CLOSING LINE. Example shape: Thank you, this was useful. Based on what you described, the part I would want first is [THING THEY NAMED AS PAINFUL]. What are the next steps?]

Triggers

You will not have time to choose a story mid sentence. Decide now. Hear the phrase on the left, lead with the story on the right.

If you hear something like thisLead with
"Tell me about a time you disagreed with an engineer"[STORY NAME]
"Walk me through a decision you got wrong"[STORY NAME]
"How do you prioritise when everything is urgent"[STORY NAME]
"Tell me about something you shipped end to end"[STORY NAME]
"How do you work with data science"[STORY NAME]
"Give me an example of influencing without authority"[STORY NAME]
"What is the hardest tradeoff you have made"[STORY NAME]
"Tell me about a metric you moved"[STORY NAME]
Stop If two stories fit, take the one you have said out loud more recently. A well rehearsed near fit beats a perfect fit you have never spoken.

Numbers

Every number you might need, isolated from the stories so you can find one without reading a paragraph. Only put numbers here you can defend if asked how you got them.

My numbers

Numbers
  • [STORY 1 HEADLINE NUMBER, plus how it was measured]
  • [STORY 2 HEADLINE NUMBER, plus how it was measured]
  • [STORY 3 HEADLINE NUMBER, plus how it was measured]
  • [SCALE NUMBER: team size, users, transactions, dollars]
  • [TIME NUMBER: how long the thing took]
Honest limit line [FOR ANY NUMBER YOU ESTIMATED: That figure is my estimate from [SOURCE], not a reported number. What I can say precisely is [THE PART YOU KNOW COLD].]

Their numbers

Numbers
  • [COMPANY SCALE FIGURE YOU VERIFIED, with the date it was published]
  • [FUNDING, HEADCOUNT, OR VOLUME FIGURE, with source date]
Stop Do not quote a number about their company unless you have the source and the date. Being wrong about their own metric in front of them is unrecoverable.

My Questions

Pick two, maybe three. In a thirty minute round you get about five minutes.

Ask these

  1. [FIRST CHOICE: about their load and what they would hand off first]
  2. [SECOND CHOICE: about direction, anchored to something specific you found]
  3. [THIRD CHOICE: about how a decision gets made when two teams disagree]
Say this out loud, word for word [YOUR WARM CLOSER QUESTION HERE. One human question, short, easy for them to answer.]
Numbers
  • Time available for questions: [MINUTES]
  • Questions to actually ask: 2, maybe 3
Honest limit line If they already answered one of these, say so and move to the backup. Asking something they covered says you were not listening.

Write down while they answer

  • How they describe the team
  • The hardest problem they name
  • Any word they repeat
  • What they seem tired of

Things that end rounds. Read this tab last before the call starts.

Landmine

[THING YOU MIGHT OVERCLAIM UNDER PRESSURE]

[THE HONEST VERSION OF WHAT YOU ACTUALLY DID.]

Honest limit line [THE ADMISSION PLUS THE PIVOT TO THE NEAREST REAL THING.]
Landmine

[NAME OR FACT YOU KEEP GETTING WRONG]

[THE CORRECT VERSION, SPELLED OUT.]

Stop Universal ones: do not fill silence after a good answer, do not answer the question you wish they had asked, do not narrate your own process out loud, do not name their vendors or their people unless they do first.
