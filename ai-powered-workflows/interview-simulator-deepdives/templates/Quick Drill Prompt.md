# Quick Drill Prompt

*A short, surgical prompt for the night before or the morning of. Not a mock round: a targeted pass over the five questions you have already fumbled, run cold, in random order, with all feedback held to the end.*

---

## When to use this instead of a full mock

*Use the full simulator when you are still building coverage: you do not know what they will ask, your stories are not settled, or you have not been graded yet. Use this drill when the opposite is true.*

Use the quick drill when:

- You have run at least one full mock and have a run log with grades.
- Your cheat sheet exists and the cards are written.
- You know exactly which questions keep going wrong.
- You have twenty to thirty minutes, not ninety.
- It is the night before or the morning of, and the goal is warming the muscle, not learning new material.

Do NOT use the quick drill when:

- You have not built the cheat sheet yet. There is nothing to load.
- You are still discovering weak spots. Discovery needs a full mock.
- You are anxious and looking for reassurance. This drill will not give it, and running it three times the night before makes performance worse.
- The round format changed. A changed format needs a rebuilt simulator, not a drill.

**Ceiling:** run it once, twice at most. Then close the laptop.

- My five most-fumbled questions: pull these from the lowest scores in the run log, not from memory. Memory picks the ones that felt bad, the log knows which ones were.

## The prompt

*Copy the whole block. Replace the bracketed placeholders. Paste it into a fresh thread so no earlier conversation softens the grading.*

```
Run a quick drill with me. Do not run a full mock and do not rebuild anything.

Load only these three files and nothing else:
- Cheat sheet: [PATH TO CHEAT SHEET]
- Rubric: [PATH TO RUBRIC]
- Run log: [PATH TO RUN LOG]

I am interviewing at [COMPANY] for [ROLE TITLE], round: [ROUND LABEL], on [DATE].

These are my five worst questions from the run log. Drill only these:

1. [QUESTION 1] (I ran long and never landed the headline)
2. [QUESTION 2] (I got vague on the numbers and hedged)
3. [QUESTION 3] (I claimed something I cannot actually defend)
4. [QUESTION 4] (I answered the question they did not ask)
5. [QUESTION 5] (I trailed off instead of ending on a clean line)

Rules for this drill:

- Randomize the order. Do not tell me the order in advance and do not tell me which of the five is coming.
- Ask one question at a time. Wait for my full answer before moving on.
- Between questions, say nothing except the next question. No coaching, no encouragement, no hints, no "good, now try this." Silence between items is the point.
- Do not grade anything until all five are done.
- Stay in the interviewer's voice while asking. Ask the question the way a real person would ask it, including any follow up pressure that person would apply, but do not add new questions outside the five.
- If I stall or say "pass," record it and move to the next one. Do not rescue me.

After the fifth answer, and only then, give me:

1. A score per question against the rubric.
2. For each one, the single sharpest fix, one sentence, phrased as something I can actually say.
3. Whether the specific failure I noted in the parentheses above happened again, yes or no, per question.
4. One overall note: the pattern across all five, if there is one.
5. Nothing else. No pep talk, no summary of my strengths.

Then append the results to the run log in the same format as the existing entries.

Start with question one now.
```

---

## After the drill

*Two minutes, then stop.*

- Which parenthetical failure repeated: [NOTE]
- The one line fix I will actually carry into the room: [NOTE]
- Card on the cheat sheet that needs sharpening (not lengthening): [CARD NAME]

*If a card came out long or mushy under pressure, cut it down tonight. Do not add explanation. A card that failed at length will fail worse at greater length.*
