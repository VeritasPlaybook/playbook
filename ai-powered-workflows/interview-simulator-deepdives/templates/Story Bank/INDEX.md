# What this is, and how it relates to your Career Brain Trust

If you have built a Career Brain Trust, this sits on top of it rather than replacing it. The two layers do different jobs, and confusing them is how people end up with a Story Bank they cannot use.

**The Career Brain Trust holds canonical written material:** verified bullets per role, the numbers, the phrasings that survived editing, organized for a resume or cover letter to draw from. It is written to be read.

**The Story Bank holds spoken narratives:** the same work re-cut into two minute stories with a headline, a decision you owned, a number you can defend under follow up, and a closing line. It is written to be said out loud, and tagged by what it proves rather than which employer it came from.

Each card carries a `Source` field pointing back to the Career Brain Trust file it came from, so you can trace a spoken claim to a verified bullet. Without a Career Brain Trust, leave the field blank and build from your resume. The card template marks every gap.

The Story Bank is the only part of this kit that is fully reusable. Companies change, rounds change, rubrics change. The seven stories you can tell cold do not.

# How to use this INDEX

1. **Always read this file in full.** It is short by design.
2. **Load selectively.** For any given mock, load the cards whose tags match what the round tests, plus Card 1 regardless. Do not load the whole folder.
3. **Never load more than five cards at once.** Past that, the assistant starts losing detail from the earliest ones, and you get vague stories back.

# The ten elements every card needs

*The checklist the assistant interrogates you against. Element five is the one most people leave out, and it separates a senior answer from a competent one.*

1. **Headline.** One line, outcome first. Then stop.
2. **Situation and stakes.** Scale, money, or risk. Why anyone cared.
3. **The ambiguity.** What was genuinely unknown or contested. If nothing was hard, it is not a story.
4. **Your assessment.** What you looked at, what you challenged, how you found the real problem.
5. **The decisions you personally made.** The options, the trade off you named, and the proof you were the one who decided. This is the seniority signal.
6. **What you built, and who did what.** Your part versus your team's part versus a vendor's part. Be precise. Vagueness here reads as credit taking.
7. **How you communicated across functions.** Who you had to bring along, and how.
8. **Result, with real numbers, before and after.** A defensible smaller number beats an impressive one that collapses under a follow up.
9. **Reflection.** What you would do differently. Not a humblebrag.
10. **Competency tags.** What this card proves, so the router knows when to reach for it.

# Rules for this folder

1. **Nothing is invented.** If a detail is not verified, the card says `NEEDS REAL DETAIL` with a specific question. A placeholder is a question, never a claim.
2. **Harden the numbers.** Every metric gets checked once and, where it was generous, corrected downward to what you can survive being pushed on. Mark the date you verified it.
3. **Append, do not rewrite.** New framings of the same story go into a Variant Framings block on the card. The canonical version stays put.
4. **One story per question.** If two cards could answer a question, the Triggers table on your cheat sheet decides which, not your instinct in the moment.
5. **Every card gets an ending.** Write the last sentence down. Trailing off is the most common delivery failure and the most fixable.
