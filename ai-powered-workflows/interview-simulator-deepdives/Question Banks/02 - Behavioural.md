# Behavioural: Question Bank

# What this round is really testing

The behavioural round makes a prediction, not an assessment. The interviewer is working out what you will do the next time something goes wrong, and the only evidence available is what you did the last time. Every question here samples your judgment in the past tense, under conditions the company expects to recreate.

Usually run by a hiring manager, a peer manager, or a trained interviewer assigned a competency from a scorecard. In a structured loop each interviewer owns two or three competencies and must leave with evidence against each. That is why the round feels repetitive: they are not curious about the story, they are filling a rubric, and they will keep digging until the box has evidence in it.

The dominant failure mode is the story with no decision in it. The candidate describes a project, a timeline, and a happy outcome, never naming a moment where two reasonable paths existed and they picked one. Without that moment there is nothing to score, so the interviewer marks it low signal and moves on while the candidate leaves thinking the round went well.

The second is drift: the question asks about conflict and the answer becomes a product tour. The third is the borrowed story, where the interesting decisions were someone else's and the candidate narrates from the audience. The fourth is the missing result, where the story stops at launch and never says what changed.

The fix is a Story Bank: a small set of real, sourced stories, each tagged to multiple competencies, numbers already checked. Interviewers ask forty different questions off maybe eight underlying competencies, so eight to twelve well-built stories cover an entire loop as long as you can re-enter each from a different door.

# How to use this bank

Copy the probes into the question bank section of your simulator prompt, identifiers exactly as written. Drill by tag, not file order, and always pick the tag with the lowest count in your tracker. Behavioural preparation fails predictably: people rehearse the three stories they like and walk into the round on a competency they have never said out loud.

Start at difficulty tier one (T1), move to tier two (T2) once you can answer without a preamble, and reach for tier three (T3) probes only in the final two sessions before the round.

Use the Situation, Action, Result (SAR) structure and hold the ratio: roughly fifteen percent situation, sixty percent action, twenty five percent result. Weak answers invert it and spend two minutes on background.

Run one story against four different probes in a single session. The skill under test is re-entry, not recall: making the same event answer a question about conflict, then ambiguity, then prioritization, without sounding reused.

# The probes

## Ownership

**BEH-01 · ownership · T1**
"Tell me about something you owned that nobody asked you to own."
*Strong answer contains:* how the candidate noticed the gap, what they did before asking permission, and evidence it survived after they moved on.

**BEH-02 · ownership · T2**
"Describe a time you took responsibility for a problem that was not yours to fix."
*Strong answer contains:* why the candidate stepped in rather than escalating, the cost of doing so, and what they handed back once it was stable.

**BEH-03 · ownership · T2**
"Tell me about a commitment you made that became much harder than expected. What did you do?"
*Strong answer contains:* the moment the candidate knew, who they told and how fast, and whether the commitment was met, renegotiated, or missed. Fast honest renegotiation scores as high as heroics.

**BEH-04 · ownership · T2**
"When did you last say no to something you were asked to do?"
*Strong answer contains:* the reasoning offered at the time, who it was said to, and what was offered instead of a flat refusal.

## Conflict

**BEH-05 · conflict · T1**
"Tell me about a conflict with a colleague."
*Strong answer contains:* a specific disagreement rather than a personality clash, what the other person wanted, and where the working relationship ended up.

**BEH-06 · conflict · T2**
"Describe a time someone blocked your work. How did you handle it?"
*Strong answer contains:* the blocker's legitimate reason (there usually was one), what the candidate learned by asking, and the change that unblocked it without escalation being the first move.

**BEH-07 · conflict · T2**
"Tell me about a conflict you handled badly."
*Strong answer contains:* a specific behaviour the candidate would not repeat, the effect on the other person, and evidence of a later situation handled differently.

**BEH-08 · conflict · T2**
"Describe working with someone whose standards were much lower than yours."
*Strong answer contains:* a check on whether the standard differed or just the priority, a direct conversation, and no contempt in the retelling.

**BEH-09 · conflict · T3**
"Tell me about a time you had to deliver hard feedback to someone more senior than you."
*Strong answer contains:* the setup (private, specific, evidence-led), the exact framing used, the reaction, and what changed. A candidate who softened it into nothing should say so.

## Failure

**BEH-10 · failure · T1**
"Tell me about a time you failed."
*Strong answer contains:* a failure with real consequences owned in the first person singular, a diagnosis of the candidate's own decision behind it, and a specific practice adopted afterward.

**BEH-11 · failure · T2**
"Describe a project that did not achieve its goal. What was your role in that?"
*Strong answer contains:* the target, the actual result, the candidate's contribution to the miss, and the earliest signal they now believe they ignored.

**BEH-12 · failure · T2**
"Tell me about a decision you made that you would reverse today."
*Strong answer contains:* what information the candidate had at the time (so the reversal is not pure hindsight), what they know now, and how they would detect the same situation earlier.

**BEH-13 · failure · T2**
"When did you break something in production or ship something that hurt users?"
*Strong answer contains:* detection time, the immediate mitigation, the communication to affected parties, and the durable fix. Function-specific: engineers, analysts, and anyone shipping to customers.

**BEH-14 · failure · T3**
"Tell me about a time you were wrong about something you were confident about."
*Strong answer contains:* the source of the misplaced confidence, what changed the candidate's mind, and how quickly they said so publicly.

**BEH-15 · failure · T3**
"What is a failure you have not fully learned from yet?"
*Strong answer contains:* genuine unresolved difficulty, the competing lessons the candidate is weighing, and no tidy bow. It punishes rehearsed answers and rewards honesty.

## Influence without authority

**BEH-16 · influence · T1**
"Tell me about a time you got something done through people who did not report to you."
*Strong answer contains:* what those people wanted independently, the specific trade or framing used, and what the candidate gave up to get the work prioritized.

**BEH-17 · influence · T2**
"Describe how you changed the mind of someone senior."
*Strong answer contains:* their stated objection, the evidence assembled for it, and the format used. Naming how the decision maker prefers to consume information scores higher.

**BEH-18 · influence · T2**
"Tell me about a time you failed to get buy-in. What happened next?"
*Strong answer contains:* an honest diagnosis (wrong audience, wrong evidence, wrong moment), what the candidate did with the work anyway, and any later attempt that landed.

**BEH-19 · influence · T2**
"How did you build credibility somewhere you were new and unknown?"
*Strong answer contains:* a first deliverable chosen for visibility and low risk, named relationships built deliberately, and a timeline.

**BEH-20 · influence · T3**
"Tell me about a time you had to sell an unpopular decision you did not make."
*Strong answer contains:* the candidate representing the decision honestly, not hiding behind leadership, naming the real trade, and what they did with the objections collected.

**BEH-21 · influence · T3**
"Describe getting two teams with opposing incentives to agree."
*Strong answer contains:* the actual incentive conflict named out loud, the mechanism used (shared metric, sequencing, escrowed scope), and whether the agreement survived contact with the next quarter.

## Ambiguity

**BEH-22 · ambiguity · T1**
"Tell me about the most ambiguous problem you have been handed."
*Strong answer contains:* the first thing the candidate did to reduce the ambiguity, the definition they wrote down, and who agreed to it.

**BEH-23 · ambiguity · T2**
"Describe a time the goal changed underneath you."
*Strong answer contains:* how the candidate found out, what they killed rather than carried forward, and how they told the people already committed.

**BEH-24 · ambiguity · T2**
"When did you have to act before you had the data you wanted?"
*Strong answer contains:* the cost of waiting, the cheapest evidence gathered in the time available, the decision, and the tripwire set to catch it being wrong.

**BEH-25 · ambiguity · T3**
"Tell me about a time nobody in the room knew the answer, including the most senior person."
*Strong answer contains:* how the candidate framed the unknown, the experiment or decomposition proposed, and how they handled the responsibility for a call made in the dark.

## Prioritization

**BEH-26 · prioritization · T1**
"How did you decide what to work on last quarter?"
*Strong answer contains:* a stated criterion applied consistently, one thing explicitly not done, and the consequence of that omission.

**BEH-27 · prioritization · T2**
"Tell me about a time you cut something people cared about."
*Strong answer contains:* the basis for the cut, the conversation with the people who cared, and what the candidate did to reduce the damage rather than absorb the anger.

**BEH-28 · prioritization · T2**
"Describe a situation with two urgent things and capacity for one."
*Strong answer contains:* the comparison made (reversibility, blast radius, cost of delay), the decision, and what was communicated to the losing side before they found out on their own.

**BEH-29 · prioritization · T3**
"Tell me about a time your prioritization was wrong and it cost the team."
*Strong answer contains:* the specific miscall, the visible cost, and the change to how the candidate now sequences work.

## Leading through others

**BEH-30 · leading-others · T2**
"Describe developing someone on your team."
*Strong answer contains:* where the person started, the specific intervention (not "I mentored them"), and a concrete change in their behaviour or scope. Applies to formal managers and informal leads.

**BEH-31 · leading-others · T2**
"Tell me about a time you had to raise the bar on someone's work."
*Strong answer contains:* how the standard was made explicit, the direct conversation, the support offered alongside the expectation, and the outcome, including where it did not work out.

**BEH-32 · leading-others · T3**
"Describe leading a team through a period when morale was low."
*Strong answer contains:* the honest cause named, what the candidate said rather than wished they could say, one thing they changed about the work itself, and what it cost them personally.

**BEH-33 · leading-others · T3**
"Tell me about a time you gave away work you were good at."
*Strong answer contains:* the reason for handing it over, the handover mechanism, the mistakes the candidate let happen, and where they drew the line on intervening.

## Customer focus

**BEH-34 · customer-focus · T1**
"Tell me about a time you changed your mind after talking to a customer or user."
*Strong answer contains:* what the candidate believed beforehand, the specific thing heard, and the concrete change made, not general appreciation of feedback.

**BEH-35 · customer-focus · T2**
"Describe a time you advocated for the customer against internal pressure."
*Strong answer contains:* the internal incentive being pushed back on, the evidence used, and the outcome, including where the candidate lost and shipped it anyway.

**BEH-36 · customer-focus · T3**
"Tell me about a time the customer asked for one thing and needed another. How did you know?"
*Strong answer contains:* how the candidate tested the underlying need rather than assuming it, what they built or recommended instead, and how the customer responded.

## Disagreement and escalation

**BEH-37 · disagreement · T2**
"Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager."
*Strong answer contains:* the substance of the disagreement, where and how it was raised, and full commitment afterward with a named trigger for revisiting.

**BEH-38 · disagreement · T3**
"When did you escalate over someone's head? Walk me through the decision to do it."
*Strong answer contains:* what was tried first, the threshold that justified escalation, whether the person was told beforehand, and the relationship cost.

## Bias for action

**BEH-39 · bias-for-action · T2**
"Tell me about the fastest you have ever gone from problem to shipped."
*Strong answer contains:* the compression method (scope cut, manual first version, borrowed infrastructure), what quality was traded, and whether the shortcut was later paid back.

**BEH-40 · bias-for-action · T3**
"Describe a time moving fast was the wrong call."
*Strong answer contains:* a specific cost incurred by speed, the signal the candidate now uses to tell the two apart, and no retreat into "we should always be careful".

# The follow-ups they will actually use

Behavioural interviewers escalate by narrowing. Each follow-up strips a layer of narration until only the candidate's own decision is left. Load all of these into the simulator and tell it to ask at least two per story.

**"What exactly did you do?"** Asked when a story is told in the plural or the passive. It repeats until a first person singular verb appears.

**"What were the other options you considered?"** A decision with one option was not a decision. This is the most common follow-up in a structured loop.

**"Who disagreed with you, and what was their argument?"** Tests whether the candidate can state the opposing case fairly. Caricaturing the other side loses points.

**"How did you know that was the right metric?"** Separates people who chose the measure from people who inherited it.

**"What was the result, and how do you know it was because of you?"** Attribution test. An honest "I cannot fully separate it from the seasonal effect" scores better than a confident overclaim.

**"What would you do differently?"** Asked after every story, success or failure. The answer must be specific enough to be a different action, not a different attitude.

**"What did that cost, and who paid it?"** Surfaces the trade-off. Stories with no cost are usually stories with no decision.

**"When did you first know it was going wrong?"** Detection speed is scored separately from response quality.

**"How long did all of that take?"** Timeline test. Compressed timelines with large outcomes get audited hard.

**"Has that ever gone badly for you?"** Asked after a confident answer about a repeated approach. It checks for a real model rather than a slogan.

# Scoring dimensions for this round

Score each one to five. Two or below should generate a defect note against the Story Bank card, not just the performance.

**Structure.** Does the answer follow SAR with the right ratio, arrive at a result, and stop. Rambling costs a point even when the content is good.

**Ownership clarity.** Is the candidate's own contribution separable from the team's within the first thirty seconds, without over-claiming.

**Decision quality.** Was there a real fork, were the alternatives named, and does the reasoning hold up when the interviewer supplies new information.

**Evidence and result.** Is there a verifiable outcome with a baseline, a measurement method, and honest attribution limits.

**Reflection.** Does the candidate name what they would change, with enough specificity to constitute a different action next time.

**Communication and presence.** Length control, no filler preamble, steadiness through three consecutive follow-ups, and a visible stop at the end of each answer.

# Landmines

**The story with no decision.** A project narrative with a timeline and a happy ending. Nothing to score, so it scores low.

**The "we" wall.** Persisting with the plural after being asked directly what the candidate did. One deflection is modesty, two is a flag.

**Drift.** Answering the conflict question with a product tour. The interviewer has a competency box to fill and an off-target story leaves it empty.

**The missing result.** Ending at the launch. If the story does not say what changed, the interviewer will assume nothing did.

**The unfalsifiable number.** Quoting a large percentage with no baseline, no measurement method, and no other causes acknowledged. It invites the hardest follow-up in the round.

**The villain.** A story where the other party is unreasonable and the candidate is right throughout. Real conflict stories contain a legitimate opposing interest.

**Fake failure.** Offering a failure that is secretly a success, or one where the candidate is the victim of someone else's mistake. It burns the failure slot and the interviewer has to ask again.

**Rehearsal voice.** Delivering at a pace and cadence that signals memorization, then falling apart when the follow-up leaves the script. Practise the re-entry, not the recitation.

**Story reuse without re-entry.** Using the same event four times without changing the door you enter through. Reuse is fine, identical retelling is not.

**The two minute setup.** Spending most of the answer on background about the industry, the company, and the org chart before reaching anything the candidate did.

**Contempt.** Any tone of superiority toward a former colleague, a former employer, or a customer. The fastest way to lose a behavioural round on a dimension nobody will name.
