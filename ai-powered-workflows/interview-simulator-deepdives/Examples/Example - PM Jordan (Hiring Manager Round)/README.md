# Example: PM Jordan, Hiring Manager Round at Northwind Payments

**Everything in this folder is fictional.** Jordan Chen is not a real person. Northwind Payments is not a real company. Devin Marchetti is not a real interviewer. Tessera, Brightline Health Systems, and Quantile Insights are invented employers. Every number is illustrative, written to be modest and defensible rather than impressive, because that is what the system produces. Do not cite anything here as fact about any real organisation.

---

## Who this person is

Jordan Chen is a Senior Product Manager (PM), roughly six years in, currently at Tessera building developer workflow tooling. Before that: two and a half years of clinician workflow at Brightline Health Systems, a regulated vertical Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) company, and a first PM role at Quantile Insights doing Business-to-Business (B2B) analytics.

Jordan already exists elsewhere in this repository. The resume guide, [Tailored, Not Templated](https://github.com/VeritasPlaybook/playbook/blob/main/ai-powered-workflows/AI%20Workflow%20for%20Resumes%20That%20Actually%20Land.md), ships an example Career Brain Trust for the same person, with the same employers, dates, and numbers. This folder is the next chapter: Jordan applied somewhere, got past the recruiter, and now has a real round on the calendar. Every story card here traces back to a bullet in that Brain Trust. Nothing was invented to make the example look better.

The earlier chapter is at [ai-powered-workflows/ai-resume-deepdives/templates/Example Career Brain Trust - PM Jordan/](https://github.com/VeritasPlaybook/playbook/tree/main/ai-powered-workflows/ai-resume-deepdives/templates/Example%20Career%20Brain%20Trust%20-%20PM%20Jordan).

## What this round is

A 45-minute hiring manager round for a Senior PM role on the Merchant Risk and Money Movement group at Northwind Payments, a fictional payments infrastructure company. One interviewer: Devin Marchetti, Director of Product, nine years in engineering before moving into product.

This round makes a good worked example because it is not a straightforward step up. Jordan is a Senior PM applying for a Senior PM title. The move trades a pivot into payments, a domain Jordan has never worked in, for a much larger surface. That is an honest, awkward, common situation, and the round turns on whether Jordan can say so out loud without inflating it or apologising for it.

## What to look at, and in what order

1. **`Run Log Excerpt.md`** first. It is the point of the system. Two graded runs, three days apart, verbatim. Run one is mediocre and finds a real hole. The cheat sheet gets a version bump. Run two is better on the dimension that was fixed and exposes a new problem that only became visible once the first was out of the way. If you read one file here, read that one.
2. **`Cheat Sheet.html`** next, because the run log keeps referring to it. Open it in a browser. It is the only artifact that exists during the real call.
3. **`_STATE.md`**, to see what the anchor file looks like when actually maintained rather than filled in once and abandoned.
4. **`Company and Role Brief.md`**, particularly the **Do not assert** section: three worked entries with safe phrasing for each.
5. **`Interviewer Dossier.md`**, particularly the headline correction at the top and the numbered directives at the bottom.
6. **`Story Bank/INDEX.md`** and the three cards, to see what a story looks like cut for speech rather than a resume.
7. **`Simulator.md`** and **`Kickoff Prompt.md`**, the machinery. Useful, not interesting.
8. **`Round Debrief.md`** last, written the afternoon of the real round.

## What is deliberately imperfect here

The Story Bank has three cards and no failure card. That is not an oversight, it is the defect the run log finds. The first run scores 2.5 on motivation specificity. The second drops half a point on a dimension nobody was working on. A worked example where the candidate nails run one would teach you nothing: the loop only earns its keep when it finds something you did not know was broken.
