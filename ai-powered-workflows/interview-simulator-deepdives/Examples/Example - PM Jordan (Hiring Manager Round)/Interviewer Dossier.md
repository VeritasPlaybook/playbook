# Interviewer Dossier: Devin Marchetti, Director of Product, Merchant Risk and Money Movement, Northwind Payments

> **Worked illustration. The person is fictional and so are the sources.** Devin Marchetti does not exist. Northwind Payments does not exist. Every source below is invented, written in the real format only so you can see what a filled dossier looks like. **Format:** Video, 45 minutes, one interviewer

## Headline correction

> **Assumed:** Director of Product means a career product person. Expect vision questions, market framing, "how would you think about this space," and a preference for candidates who talk in outcomes and narratives.
>
> **Actually:** Devin spent nine years in engineering before product. Backend engineer for six years, then engineering manager for three on the team that owned settlement, then across into product in 2023. The move was sideways within the same problem area, not a promotion out of it. Devin still writes the technical design review notes for the group, and both public talks are engineering talks, not product talks.
>
> **Consequence for the round:** my instinct would have been to open with market framing, the "payments infrastructure is where workflow problems get expensive" line I like. That would have landed as a candidate performing product-ness at somebody who has read a thousand design documents. Devin will be listening for whether I can describe the system underneath a product decision without hand-waving, and whether I know what I do not know about it.

## What they are likely to test, and why (INFERRED)

1. **Likely test:** whether I can describe a system, not just a product decision.
   **Why I think so (INFERRED):** nine years in engineering, still writes the design review notes. A product manager who says "we improved the reconciliation experience" and cannot say what reconciles against what gets one polite follow-up and then a quiet downgrade.

2. **Likely test:** how I behave at the edge of what I know, given that I have never worked in payments.
   **Why I think so (INFERRED):** engineering managers who move into product tend to have strong feelings about confident wrongness, having spent years cleaning it up. Devin will find the edge of my payments knowledge inside ten minutes. The test is not whether the edge exists, it is what I do when we arrive at it.

3. **Likely test:** whether the weekly one to one would be spent on the work or on me.
   **Why I think so (INFERRED):** this is a direct-report hire into a group formed five months ago out of two separate teams. Someone who needs a lot of context handed to them is expensive right now in a way they would not be in a settled team.

4. **Likely test:** whether I understand that a lateral title move needs a reason.
   **Why I think so (INFERRED):** I am a Senior Product Manager (PM) applying for a Senior PM role. Devin took a sideways move in 2023 and will have a well-formed view about what makes one make sense. This could go either way: a shared experience, or a place where I sound like I have not thought it through.

## What wins them

- Naming the failure mode before naming the fix. Describing what breaks, then what you did about it.
- Saying "I do not know" in four words and then what you would do to find out, without a paragraph of throat-clearing.
- Talking about verification. How you knew a thing worked, not just that it worked. The June 2025 post is essentially about verification.
- Being precise about the boundary of what you owned. Devin managed engineers for three years and has heard every version of taking credit for a team's work.

## What loses them

- Product vocabulary standing in for detail. "Alignment," "north star," "customer obsession." Not sins in general, specifically expensive here.
- A vision answer to a mechanics question.
- Claiming payments knowledge I do not have, at any size. Even a small overclaim: the follow-up is one question deep and Devin spent six years inside the actual system.
- Treating the exception queue as an obviously bad thing that should be automated away. Somebody built that queue on purpose and it catches things.

## The quiet question in their head

> **Their unspoken doubt:** "This person has never worked in payments. Am I going to spend two quarters teaching them the domain before they are useful to me, in a group that was reorganised five months ago and does not have two quarters spare?"
>
> **How I disarm it without naming it:** I do not wait to be asked. Early, on the first relevant question, I name the gap in one sentence and attach evidence of the pattern rather than a promise. Something close to: "I have not worked in payments. What I have done twice is come into a domain where the rules are not negotiable and the users had good reasons not to trust the software. At Brightline I was useful by month three and I can tell you what month one and two actually cost." The move converts an unknown risk into a known and previously survived risk. A promise to learn fast is worth nothing. A previous instance of learning fast, with the cost stated, is worth something.

## What this means for my prep

1. Devin came up through engineering and still writes design review notes, therefore I rewrite the opening 25 seconds to lead with a system I built rather than an outcome I drove, and test whether I can say what the workflow automation engine did to a queue without using the word "streamlined."

2. Devin's writing spends more space on verification than on the new system, therefore I add a "how I knew it worked" line to all three story cards, including the measurement method, before the next mock. This is the line I would otherwise have improvised badly.

3. Devin will locate the edge of my payments knowledge inside ten minutes, therefore I write the boundary line word for word on the cheat sheet and say it out loud until it comes out level rather than apologetic. The failure mode is not admitting the gap, it is admitting it in a tone that invites reassurance.

4. Devin took a lateral move in 2023, therefore I prepare a direct answer to "is this a step up, sideways, or down" that says sideways, plainly, with the surface trade named. No relabelling. If I sell a lateral move as a promotion to somebody who made one on purpose, I lose the room for the rest of the call.

5. Devin managed engineers for three years and has heard every version of credit-taking, therefore I write the explicit ownership split for the automation engine story: what I decided, what the two engineers designed, what the design partner customers pushed back on. Three lines, on the card, before the next run.
