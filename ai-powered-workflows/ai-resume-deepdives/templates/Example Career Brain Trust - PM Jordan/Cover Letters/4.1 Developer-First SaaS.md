### 4.1 Developer-First Software-as-a-Service Archetype

**Tags:** `#DeveloperTools` `#Platform` `#Builder` `#Technical`
**Use when:** applying to a developer-facing Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) platform (think Linear, GitHub, Vercel, PlanetScale, Sentry, or any similar product where engineers are both the buyer and the user).

---

#### Opener variants

- "If you are looking for a Senior Product Manager (PM) who has shipped developer workflow tooling used by 12,000-plus engineers and knows when the Artificial Intelligence (AI) should act vs. ask, I'm your candidate."
- "If you are looking for a Lead PM who builds with engineers and not for them, I'm your candidate."

---

#### Reason headers used in this archetype

- **"I ship for working engineering teams under load, not demo conditions."**
  Elaboration: At Tessera I own the developer workflow surface used by 12,000-plus engineers across 200-plus engineering organizations. The workflow automation engine I built was adopted by ~70 percent of paid customers within 90 days. The decision driver was not the feature graph; it was that I shipped a working version inside our own engineering team for six weeks before we touched a paid customer.

- **"Integrations breadth is the expansion lever."**
  Elaboration: I owned the integrations roadmap at Tessera and shipped 6 new connectors (GitHub Enterprise, GitLab self-hosted, Jira Data Center, Linear, Sentry, PagerDuty) that drove a 19 percent expansion in average accounts per paying customer. In developer tooling, the platform that wins the integration sprawl wins the account.

- **"When the Artificial Intelligence acts vs. when it asks."**
  Elaboration: I shipped the AI task assistant in Tessera and drove a 31 percent week-one retention lift. The lift came from the product decision, not the model choice: the assistant defers when it's not confident and acts only when it can be reversed in one click. That heuristic is portable across any AI product I'd touch.

- **"Engineering empathy from first principles."**
  Elaboration: I run a weekly customer council with engineering leads at 8 high-Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) accounts. The council surfaced 4 major roadmap items that shipped within the year. The product decisions that matter most are the ones an engineer would have made themselves; my job is to find them, prioritize them, and clear the path.

---

#### Closing variants

- "If you'd like to compare notes on where developer workflow goes next and where I could plug in fastest, I'd welcome the conversation."

---

#### Notes and variants

- Tone: direct, builder-voiced, technical depth signal. Avoid corporate-PM language. Avoid words like "synergy", "leverage", "stakeholder" if there's a more direct phrasing.
- Lead with the most-specific platform-scale credibility (Tessera) and the AI surface. Healthcare (Brightline) is a supporting line, not a lead.
- Numbers help. Specific products help more (GitHub Enterprise, GitLab self-hosted, Jira Data Center, Linear, Sentry, PagerDuty by name) because the reader knows the integration cost.
- Avoid: leaning on credentials over output. Reforge alum is a fine line in education; it is not a reason header.

Variant framings specifically used in this archetype:

- "Shipped real-time code review surfaces inside the developer editor, used by 12,000-plus engineers; designed for working teams under load, not demo conditions." [Lead PM at Linear-style company application; reused in two later applications]
