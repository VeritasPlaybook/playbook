### 4.2 Vertical Software-as-a-Service Archetype

**Tags:** `#VerticalSaaS` `#Domain` `#Usability` `#Compliance` `#Trust`
**Use when:** applying to a vertical Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) platform in healthcare, legal, construction, education, financial services, or any industry where the buyer and the daily user differ and trust is the product.

---

#### Opener variants

- "If you are looking for a Senior Product Manager (PM) who has shipped inside a regulated vertical Software-as-a-Service environment and knows that compliance and usability are the same workstream, I'm your candidate."
- "If you are looking for a PM who treats clinician (or attorney, or contractor, or teacher) workflows as the actual product, not the buyer demo, I'm your candidate."

---

#### Reason headers used in this archetype

- **"Trust is the product in vertical Software-as-a-Service."**
  Elaboration: At Brightline Health Systems I shipped Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)-compliant patient messaging in 6 weeks vs. the internal 12-week benchmark. The compression came from running design review in parallel with technical and legal review, not after. In vertical SaaS, every quarter you save on compliance velocity is a quarter your competitor doesn't.

- **"Discovery happens in the room, not on a survey."**
  Elaboration: At Brightline I ran 14 clinician interviews and 3 ambulatory observation sessions for the post-visit summary feature. The friction I saw in the room (clinicians dictating to a phone while charting on a laptop) never appeared in survey data. The feature that shipped solved the real problem because I'd watched it happen, not just heard about it.

- **"I cut clinician documentation time by roughly 40 percent across 4 ambulatory care pilots."**
  Elaboration: The documentation workflow redesign I led at Brightline reduced average charting time from 12 minutes to 7 minutes per patient. The win was less about the workflow and more about what got removed (5 fields that no one used) and what got added back (one autocomplete primitive that worked offline). Vertical SaaS rewards subtraction.

- **"Integration breadth is how vertical Software-as-a-Service crosses the chasm."**
  Elaboration: I grew the Electronic Health Records (EHR) integration module from 0 to 23 supported systems in 18 months at Brightline, including the three majors (Epic, Cerner, athenahealth) plus 20 community-hospital systems. The community-hospital long tail mattered as much as the majors because that's where most of the market lives.

---

#### Closing variants

- "If you'd like to talk about how the clinician (or attorney, or teacher) workflow could ship faster on your team, I'd welcome the conversation."

---

#### Notes and variants

- Tone: domain-empathetic first, technical second. Vertical SaaS hiring managers screen out PMs who lead with platform language; they want to know you understand the actual day of the user.
- Lead with Brightline. Tessera is a supporting line that signals platform fluency, not the lead.
- Numbers help, but only the user-facing ones (clinician time saved, daily active usage, design review cycle time). Avoid leading with internal-engineering metrics in this archetype.
- Avoid: pretending domain depth I don't have. If the company is in legal or construction, I'll acknowledge the domain change explicitly and lean on the transferable lessons (compliance velocity, in-the-room research, integration breadth) rather than fake fluency.

Variant framings specifically used in this archetype:

- "Cut clinician charting time by roughly 40 percent across 4 ambulatory care pilots through a documentation workflow redesign anchored in direct clinician observation, not survey data." [Application to a clinician-facing vertical SaaS Senior PM role; reused in three later applications]
- "Partnered with Compliance, Privacy, and Legal to ship Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)-compliant patient messaging in 6 weeks vs. an internal benchmark of 12; the compression came from running design review in parallel with technical review, not after it." [Application to a regulated vertical SaaS role; reused in two later applications]
