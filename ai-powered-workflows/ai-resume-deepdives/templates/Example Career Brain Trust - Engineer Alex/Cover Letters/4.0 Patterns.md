### 4.0 Recurring Patterns (Alex's engineering-letter house style)

Engineering cover letters tend to be shorter and more pragmatic than Product Manager letters. Hiring managers screen them quickly looking for technical-depth signal and a clean code portfolio link. The pattern I use:

1. **Opening hook.** One sentence that names the role and signals fit. Format: "If you are looking for a backend engineer who has [specific primitive], I'm your candidate."
2. **Header.** "A few things you might want to know."
3. **Three bolded technical-depth signals**, each followed by a two-sentence elaboration anchored in a specific employer and a specific primitive (idempotency, exactly-once, sharded Postgres, etc.).
4. **Code portfolio anchor.** A single sentence pointing to the GitHub profile or a specific repository the reader can open to see how I actually write code.
5. **Closing.** "If you'd like to walk through any of the above, I'd welcome the conversation. My GitHub is [URL]."

Notes:

- The total letter is 200 to 350 words. Engineering hiring managers do not want to read 600 words of prose. They want signal.
- The bolded headlines are the technical-depth claims. The elaboration is where I name the specific primitive and the specific outcome.
- The code-portfolio anchor is non-negotiable. Every cover letter I send includes a direct link to the public artifact the reader could look at.

---

#### When I break the pattern

- When the application is a take-home or async code test as the first step. Then the cover letter is a one-paragraph "hello, here is my GitHub" message and the take-home does the work.
- When the company is explicitly hiring for a non-IC role I want (rare; I want IC). Then the letter shifts toward mentorship and Request-for-Comments (RFC) authorship.
