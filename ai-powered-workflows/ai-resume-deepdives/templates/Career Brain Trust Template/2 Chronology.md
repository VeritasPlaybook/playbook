# 2. Chronology

This is the canonical career chronology. One table, one source of truth. Every Experience file's dates must match the row in this table.

If anything disagrees, this file wins. If you discover a date is wrong, fix it here first, then update the Experience file to match.

---

## Section 2B: Data Discrepancy Resolutions

Over the course of a career, you'll run into small data conflicts. A role overlapped by a month with the next one. A title changed midway through an employer's reorganization. A start date in your LinkedIn profile disagrees with your resume.

Resolve them once, here, then enforce the canonical version everywhere.

A working format:

### Discrepancy 1

- **Question:** [What was unclear, e.g., "Did the WidgetCo role start in May 2018 or June 2018?"]
- **Resolution:** [The canonical answer, e.g., "May 2018, per the offer letter."]
- **Source:** [Where the canonical answer came from, e.g., "PDF of original offer letter, May 1 2018."]
- **Last verified:** [YYYY-MM-DD]

### Discrepancy 2

- **Question:** [Description]
- **Resolution:** [Answer]
- **Source:** [Source]
- **Last verified:** [YYYY-MM-DD]

Add as needed. The reason to keep this section is that future-you will run into the same question again in six months and want the answer without re-investigating.

## Section 2C: Chronology Rules

Optional. Some careers have rules about what gets included on a resume. These belong here as one-liners so your skills can honor them.

Examples:

- "Never include any role that predates the relevant career pivot date."
- "Never include the early-career engineering roles on a resume; they appear only in LinkedIn for chronology continuity."
- "Always group roles at the same employer under one block (e.g., a promotion from Senior to Lead at the same company appears as a single dated block with two sub-roles)."

Add your own as you discover them.
