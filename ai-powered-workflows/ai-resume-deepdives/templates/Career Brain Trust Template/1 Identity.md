# 1. Identity

This file holds your professional identity: your headline, your summary, and a library of alternate positioning statements you can mix and match into cover letters.

The point of keeping all of these in one place is that your AI can pick the right opening line for any given Job Description without you re-writing it from scratch every time.

---

## Section 1A: LinkedIn Headline

Your headline is the single most-read sentence in your professional life. It shows up under your name in every search result, every comment, every connection request. Keep it tight.

A working format:

> [Function] at the intersection of [Domain 1] and [Domain 2] | [What you build or do] | [Signal of credibility or specialty]

Replace with your own:

> **[Your headline here]**

Notes about your headline:

- [What it emphasizes and why]
- [What you tried that did not work, if any]
- [Last refresh date]

---

## Section 1B: Professional Summary

Two or three sentences. The summary is what shows up under "About" on LinkedIn and what your AI quotes back when a cover letter calls for a strong opening paragraph.

Two paragraphs is usually enough. Lead with what you do today, then back into what you have done that proves you can do it. Avoid generic adjectives. Use concrete nouns and verbs.

> [Your summary here.]
>
> [Second paragraph here, if needed.]

## Section 1C: Positioning Library

A library of alternate openers and positioning statements, tagged by angle. These are the building blocks your AI pulls from when drafting a cover letter or a tailored resume header.

Each entry has the same shape: a short positioning statement, the tag it answers to, and a one-line note on when to use it.

Add to this library every time you write a cover opener that lands. Promote a variant framing here whenever you find one that consistently works.

### Builder framing

> [Positioning statement that emphasizes shipping, building, zero-to-one.]
**Tags:** `#Build` `#0to1`
**Use when:** the role explicitly calls for builders, founding team experience, or zero-to-one work.

### Scale framing

> [Positioning statement that emphasizes operating at scale, large-organization wins.]
**Tags:** `#Scale` `#Enterprise`
**Use when:** the role is at a large company or explicitly mentions scale, throughput, or large user bases.

### Domain-specialist framing

> [Positioning statement that emphasizes deep domain expertise in your strongest field.]
**Tags:** `#[Your domain tag]` `#Expert`
**Use when:** the role is in your specialist domain and wants a domain expert, not a generalist.

### Generalist framing

> [Positioning statement that emphasizes range and breadth across domains.]
**Tags:** `#Generalist` `#CrossDomain`
**Use when:** the role explicitly wants a generalist, or the team is small and the person needs to wear several hats.

### Leadership framing

> [Positioning statement that emphasizes people leadership, hiring, mentoring.]
**Tags:** `#Leadership` `#Manager`
**Use when:** the role is a manager-of-managers or has explicit team-building responsibilities.

### Hands-on framing

> [Positioning statement that emphasizes individual contribution, technical depth, not delegating.]
**Tags:** `#IC` `#HandsOn`
**Use when:** the role is an individual contributor at a senior or staff level and the company values hands-on work.

### Mission-aligned framing

> [Positioning statement that ties your motivation to a particular mission or impact area.]
**Tags:** `#Mission` `#Impact`
**Use when:** the company leads with mission language and the role's hiring manager will be reading for values fit.

### Pivot framing

> [Positioning statement that addresses an industry or function pivot head-on.]
**Tags:** `#Pivot` `#CareerChange`
**Use when:** the role represents a deliberate move and you want to acknowledge it directly rather than have the reader infer it.

Add as many more framings as you need. The point is that when your AI is drafting an opener for a Job Description, it can pick the framing that matches the role rather than defaulting to your one canonical opener every time.
