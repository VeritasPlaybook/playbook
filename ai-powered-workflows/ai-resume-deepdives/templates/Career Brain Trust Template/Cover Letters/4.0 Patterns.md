### 4.0 Recurring Patterns (your cover letter "house style")

This file holds the recurring skeleton your cover letters share, regardless of archetype. The point is to lock the shape once so that every tailored cover letter starts from the same scaffold, then varies the building blocks per archetype.

A working four-part skeleton most readers can adopt:

1. **Opening hook.** One sentence that names the role and signals fit fast. Format: "If you are looking for [a specific role description that matches the Job Description], I am your guy/your candidate."
2. **Header.** A single line that transitions from the hook into the reasons. Format: "What makes me your ideal candidate?"
3. **Three or four bolded reason headlines**, each followed by a two-to-four-sentence elaboration. The reasons are the swappable building blocks. The same reason can recur across many archetypes if it lands well.
4. **Closing.** One sentence that invites the reader to engage. Format: "If you feel I would be an asset to [their mission, team, or product line], please reach out; I would love to discuss how I can add value."

Notes on this skeleton:

- The opener is the most-read sentence. Spend disproportionate time on it.
- The bolded headlines are the units of memory. The hiring manager skims them before reading the elaboration. They should each stand on their own as a credibility signal.
- The closing should feel warm, not transactional. The reader will not remember it, but they will remember the tone it left.

Tailor the elaboration per Job Description; keep the skeleton.

---

#### When to break the pattern

The pattern is a default, not a law. Break it when:

- The role explicitly asks for a particular cover letter format (e.g., "tell us in 200 words why you want to work here"). Honor the explicit ask.
- The opener feels forced for a creative-shaped role (design, brand marketing, content). Lead with the strongest creative line instead.
- You have a one-of-one credibility hook (e.g., "I used your product to launch my last company"). Lead with that.

If you break the pattern often enough that it stops being the default, update this file.
