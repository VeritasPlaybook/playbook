### 4.2 Retail and Direct-to-Consumer Digital Transformation Archetype

**Tags:** `#Retail` `#DTC` `#Brand` `#Lifecycle` `#Transformation`
**Use when:** applying to a hybrid retail or Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) brand in the middle of its own digital transformation. These companies value the bilingual marketer who can connect physical retail and digital growth.

---

#### Opener variants

- "I came up in brand and retail marketing and pivoted into lifecycle and Product-Led Growth (PLG) over the last three years. The brand foundation is what I think makes the lifecycle work; here's how that shows up at Cordata Outfitters."
- "If you are looking for a Director-level marketer who can speak both retail and digital fluently, and who has shipped a Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) mobile-app lifecycle program at a retail-rooted company from zero, I'm your candidate."

---

#### Reason headers used in this archetype

- **"I've built Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) growth inside a retail-rooted company."**
  Elaboration: At Cordata Outfitters I built and own the mobile-app lifecycle program from zero; the program now drives roughly 18 percent of quarterly Direct-to-Consumer revenue. The retail-store partnership is the underrated channel; the in-store quick-response code drove 22 percent of the install base. Retail and digital are not separate motions; they reinforce each other when you set up the measurement correctly.

- **"Integrated campaigns are the discipline that ports across both halves."**
  Elaboration: At Mosswood & Co. I owned the integrated 2022 holiday campaign across broadcast, print, in-store, and email; the campaign drove the highest in-store traffic week the brand had recorded since 2019. The discipline (every channel landing at the customer in the same week telling the same story) is the same discipline behind the Direct-to-Consumer mobile-app lifecycle program at Cordata.

- **"I built the lifecycle attribution model Cordata's broader marketing team now uses."**
  Elaboration: The model marries last-click for sanity with multi-touch for new-channel decisions. For a company in the middle of its own transformation, attribution credibility is the prerequisite for making any of the digital investment decisions you are about to make.

- **"Brand foundation is the lever under every lifecycle decision I make."**
  Elaboration: Emails that convert and push notifications that retain are not separate from the brand. They are the brand showing up in 60-character form. The companies that win at digital transformation are the ones where the brand foundation is still the lever under the lifecycle work.

---

#### Closing variants

- "If you'd like to talk through how the brand foundation and the lifecycle work both show up on day one, or how the retail-store partnership channel could light up faster, I'd welcome the conversation."

---

#### Notes and variants

- Tone: lead with the brand-foundation roots as a feature, not a defense. The traditional roots are precisely what these companies are missing in their growth hires.
- Lead with Cordata for the digital credibility, but allocate equal space to Mosswood and Aldercroft. Both halves matter for this archetype.
- Name the retail-store partnership channel explicitly. Most growth-marketing hires cannot speak to it; that is the differentiator.
- The integrated-campaign framing translates better than "omnichannel" in this archetype. "Omnichannel" is corporate-speak; "every channel landing at the customer in the same week telling the same story" is the actual work.

Variant framings specifically used in this archetype:

- "The retail-store partnership is the underrated channel; the in-store quick-response code drove 22 percent of the install base." [Application to a hybrid retail-digital Director role; reused in two later applications]
- "Every channel landing at the customer in the same week telling the same story" from Mosswood's 2022 holiday campaign. [Reused across three applications in this archetype]
