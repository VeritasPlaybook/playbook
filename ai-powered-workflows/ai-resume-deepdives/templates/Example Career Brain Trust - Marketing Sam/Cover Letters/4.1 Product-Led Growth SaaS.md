### 4.1 Product-Led Growth Software-as-a-Service Archetype

**Tags:** `#SaaS` `#PLG` `#Growth` `#Lifecycle` `#Pivot`
**Use when:** applying to a pure-play Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) or Product-Led Growth (PLG) company, including consumer apps, freemium SaaS, and growth-led startups. The pivot from traditional marketing is the obvious question; the cover letter must name it.

---

#### Opener variants

- "I'm three years into a deliberate pivot from traditional brand-and-retail marketing into lifecycle and Product-Led Growth (PLG). The pivot is real, recent, and earned. Here's what I think you'll find useful."
- "If you are looking for a Director-level marketer who has built a Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) mobile-app lifecycle program from zero and treats brand foundation as load-bearing rather than ornamental, I'm your candidate."

---

#### Reason headers used in this archetype

- **"I've shipped the Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) mobile-app lifecycle program at Cordata Outfitters from zero."**
  Elaboration: I came in three years ago as a brand marketer and built the lifecycle program for the company's then-pre-launch mobile app. The program now drives roughly 18 percent of quarterly Direct-to-Consumer revenue, lifted month-one mobile retention by 28 percent through a re-sequenced onboarding flow plus behaviorally-triggered push notifications, and grew the app to roughly 180,000 monthly active users in 28 months.

- **"I built the lifecycle attribution model Cordata's broader marketing team now uses."**
  Elaboration: The model marries last-click for sanity with a multi-touch view for new-channel investment decisions. It survived contact with the finance team, which is the actual test. Attribution work is the unglamorous part of growth that compounds the most.

- **"Brand foundation is the lever under every lifecycle decision I make."**
  Elaboration: The integrated campaign work I did at Mosswood & Co. (broadcast, print, in-store, email landing in the same week with the same story) is the foundation under the lifecycle work I do at Cordata. Emails that convert and push notifications that retain are not separate from the brand; they are the brand showing up in 60-character form.

- **"I ran 41 A/B tests in the last 18 months."**
  Elaboration: The discipline behind the experiment cadence came from the creative-brief work I did agency-side at Aldercroft Media (one-page, written-and-defended, clear hypothesis). The discipline is what makes experimentation actually useful instead of busy.

---

#### Closing variants

- "If you'd like to talk through the attribution model, the experiment log, or how the brand foundation shows up under the lifecycle work, I'd welcome the conversation."

---

#### Notes and variants

- Tone: name the pivot in the first sentence. Do not bury it. The reader will spot it and want me to address it.
- Lead with Cordata. Mosswood and Aldercroft are supporting context unless the JD names brand specifically.
- Use the "deliberate pivot" framing rather than "transition" or "evolution"; the deliberate framing is more credible than the soft language.
- The Reforge certificates earn a mention in the body, not the headline. They are pivot evidence, not pivot credentials.

Variant framings specifically used in this archetype:

- "Built and own the Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) mobile-app lifecycle program at Cordata Outfitters from zero; the program now drives roughly 18 percent of quarterly Direct-to-Consumer revenue. The work is the pivot evidence I bring with me." [Application to a freemium-SaaS Director of Lifecycle role; reused in two later applications]
- "Lifted month-one mobile retention by 28 percent at Cordata through a re-sequenced onboarding flow plus behaviorally-triggered push notifications. The win came from treating retention as a sequencing problem rather than a copy problem." [Application to a mobile-app-first SaaS company; reused in three later applications]
