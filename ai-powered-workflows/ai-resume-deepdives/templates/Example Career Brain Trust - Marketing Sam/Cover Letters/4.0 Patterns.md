### 4.0 Recurring Patterns (Sam's cover letter house style)

A consistent four-part skeleton across my cover letters, with a pivot-aware opening pattern that I lean on for any Software-as-a-Service or pure Product-Led Growth application:

1. **Opening hook.** Names the role and signals fit. For Software-as-a-Service or Product-Led Growth (PLG) roles, the opening also names the pivot ("three years into a deliberate pivot from brand-and-retail to lifecycle and PLG") so the reader does not have to wonder.
2. **Header.** "What I think you'll find useful in my background."
3. **Three or four bolded reason headlines**, each followed by a two-to-four-sentence elaboration. For pivoting applications, at least one reason must be a digital or Product-Led Growth credibility line from Cordata Outfitters; at least one must be a transferable-skill bridge from the traditional roots.
4. **Closing.** Honest about excitement. Format: "If you'd like to talk through how the brand foundation and the lifecycle work both show up on day one, I'd welcome the conversation."

---

#### The pivot-aware opening pattern

For applications to Software-as-a-Service or Product-Led Growth roles where the pivot is the obvious question:

> "I'm three years into a deliberate pivot from traditional brand-and-retail marketing into lifecycle and Product-Led Growth (PLG). The pivot is real, recent, and earned. Here's what I think you'll find useful."

This pattern works because:

- It acknowledges the pivot in the first sentence; the reader does not have to guess.
- It names "deliberate" and "earned" so the reader does not assume the pivot is opportunistic.
- It transitions to "here's what I think you'll find useful" so the rest of the letter delivers value rather than defending the pivot.

For applications to hybrid retail-digital companies where the traditional roots are a feature:

> "I came up in brand and retail marketing and pivoted into lifecycle and Product-Led Growth (PLG) over the last three years. The brand foundation is what I think makes the lifecycle work; here's how that shows up at Cordata Outfitters."

This pattern works because:

- It leads with the traditional roots as a feature, not a defense.
- It frames the pivot as additive, not as a course correction.
- It transitions to current work as evidence rather than narrative.

---

#### When I break the pattern

- When the role explicitly asks for a specific cover letter format (e.g., "describe yourself in 200 words"). I honor the format.
- When I have a one-of-one credibility hook (e.g., "I have been a daily user of your app since 2023"). I lead with that.
